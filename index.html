About

Add photo.jpg to data/

My research journey sits at the intersection of Earth sciences, environmental health, and energy transitions—shaped by a simple question: how do the materials beneath our feet shape human health and the future of our planet?

I began in coal geology and mineralogy, drawn to the Late Permian coals of Yunnan. That work led me to Xuan Wei, a county with among the highest lung cancer rates in China, especially among women who burn local coal indoors for cooking and heating. There, I traced the link between Fe-rich chamosite and berthierine in the coal and carcinogenic mineral particles released during combustion—nanometer-sized particles that trigger oxidative stress and malignant transformation in bronchial cells. Understanding that chain—from mineral formation to indoor exposure to disease—drove home how deeply geology and human health are intertwined.

Today, I’m based at The University of Hong Kong, where my work has expanded from understanding environmental health risks to designing solutions. I collaborate on pathways to scale green hydrogen and carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS) in China—including integrated cement-methanol co-production—to reduce reliance on fossil fuels and curb emissions. Bridging Earth sciences with energy transitions reflects my view that both diagnosis and remedy matter: we need to understand harm, then build alternatives.
